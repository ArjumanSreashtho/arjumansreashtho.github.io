Pakhi Phurr Privacy Policy

Effective 25 November 2026

English বাংলা हिन्दी

The short version

  • Pakhi Phurr is a bird puzzle game made for children and families.
  • We do not collect personal information from anyone. There are no accounts, no sign-up, no chat and no servers of our own.
  • Progress, stars, album cards, coins and settings are saved only on the device.
  • Ads come from Google AdMob. Every ad request is marked as being for a child, so ads are not personalised and are limited to content rated for general audiences (G). The game does not use the advertising ID.
  • The only purchase, “Remove ads”, is handled by Google Play and sits behind a question for grown-ups.

1. Who we are

Pakhi Phurr (“the game”) is made by PlayFlex, an independent game developer in Bangladesh (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what happens to information when the game is used on Android, installed from Google Play, and in any web version. Questions are welcome at arjumansreashtho@gmail.com.

2. Information saved on the device

So that a player can continue where they left off, the game saves on the device: level progress and stars, the bird album cards won, the daily puzzle calendar and streak, Sky mode boards cleared, coins and hints, settings (sound, music, vibration, display, reduced motion, language), counters that space out ads between levels, and whether “Remove ads” was bought.

Our code never sends this off the device: we have no server, and the game has no analytics or crash-reporting software. Daily and Sky puzzles are made on the device, without a connection. The bird calls are recordings that ship inside the game; the game never uses the microphone. Everything saved can be deleted at any time by clearing the game’s storage in the phone’s settings or uninstalling the game. If device backup is turned on, Android may include this save in the device backup to the Google Account; we cannot access those backups.

3. Advertising (Google AdMob)

The game shows ads through Google AdMob, a Families self-certified ads SDK. Short videos are always optional: the player chooses to watch one for a hint, an extra heart or double coins. A full-screen ad may appear between campaign levels from level 10 on, at most once every three levels, never during play, never in the daily puzzle or Sky mode, and not when “Remove ads” was bought. There are no banner ads.

Because children use the game, every ad request is tagged for child-directed treatment and for users under the age of consent, with a maximum ad content rating of G, and asks for non-personalised ads. Under these settings Google does not show personalised ads or remarketing. The Android app also removes the advertising ID permission, so the advertising ID is not read or sent.

To show and count ads and to prevent fraud, the Google Mobile Ads SDK still processes some technical data, such as the IP address (to estimate the general region), the device type and OS version, app and SDK performance, and ad interactions such as views and taps (Google’s data disclosure). Google handles this as described in the Google Privacy Policy. AdMob gives us only totals, such as how many ads were shown, which do not identify anyone.

If Google requires a privacy choice for a region, a Privacy options row appears in the game’s Settings.

4. Purchases (Google Play)

The game offers one optional one-time purchase, “Remove ads”. Before the purchase screen opens, the game asks a short multiplication meant for grown-ups. Google Play processes the payment; we never see card or payment details. Google Play tells the game on the device that the purchase is complete, so it can turn off ads between levels and restore the purchase later.

5. Permissions

The Android app does not ask for location, contacts, camera, microphone, photos or files. It uses only permissions Android grants without a prompt: internet and network state (ads and purchases), Google Play billing, and vibration (which can be turned off in Settings). It does not use the advertising ID.

6. Links out of the game

The only link out of the game is this privacy policy, on the About screen. It too waits behind the question for grown-ups.

7. Web version

In a web version, progress is kept in the browser’s local storage on the device. It shows no real ads (videos are simulated) and offers no purchases. The website host may keep standard technical logs, such as IP addresses, under its own privacy statement.

8. Children

Pakhi Phurr is designed for children and families and follows the Google Play Families policy. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children, and in fact we do not collect personal information from anyone. Parents and guardians can write to us at any time with questions, and can delete everything the game saved by clearing its storage or uninstalling it.

9. Keeping data and security

We hold no data about players, so there is nothing on our side to keep or delete. Google states that the data its ads SDK collects is encrypted in transit. Data saved on the device is protected by the device’s own security.

10. Changes to this policy

If the game changes in a way that affects privacy, we will update this page and its date before the change is released, and update the game’s Data safety information on Google Play.
